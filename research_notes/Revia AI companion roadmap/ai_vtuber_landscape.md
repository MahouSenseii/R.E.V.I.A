# AI VTuber Landscape and Technical Stack (as of Sept 2026)

Scope: how leading AI VTubers (above all Neuro-sama) and open-source AI VTuber projects work end to end (avatar, voice use, chat, streaming integration, safety, formats), written for a solo developer building "Revia", a local-first Windows C++20/Qt 6 companion/VTuber that starts with Live2D, later moves to VRM, and streams to Twitch and YouTube.

Research notes:
- GitHub API and git access to third-party repos were blocked in this session. Star counts and licenses come from each repo's GitHub page, read with WebFetch in late Sept 2026. Last-commit dates come from each repo's `commits.atom` feed. Treat star counts as approximate.
- The web-search budget ran out partway through. Some sub-questions (prompt-injection incidents, a verified roster of other AI VTubers) stayed thin and are listed under Gaps.
- Community-sourced claims (Fandom wikis, a third-party research compilation, search snippets) are labelled that way.

## 1. Neuro-sama (Vedal987): what is publicly known about her architecture, and why she works as entertainment

### Takeaway
Very little of Neuro-sama's stack is officially documented. Publicly attested items:
- a custom fine-tune of an undisclosed open-weight LLM, run on Vedal's own GPUs and retrained offline from curated stream transcripts
- an Azure TTS voice (community-sourced)
- a separate singing system
- an output filter that visibly replaces blocked lines with "Filtered"
- games driven through a structured WebSocket action API (Neuro SDK), not raw keyboard or mouse control

Her success comes less from the tech than from the format: a chaotic, fast-talking character; a human straight man (Vedal); a sibling foil (Evil Neuro); constant collabs; and subathon events that turned her into one of Twitch's biggest subscription channels in 2025-2026.

### Cited Findings

**History and avatar**
- Origin: created in 2018 as an osu! bot, which was finished in 2022. It beat top player mrekk in a 1v1 and ranked first on a private leaderboard (it was ineligible for the public one). She relaunched with a voice and avatar on Twitch on Dec 19, 2022. By June 2023 she averaged about 5,700 viewers and had become Vedal's full-time job — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuro-sama)
- Avatar history:
  - 2022-23: Live2D's free sample model "Hiyori Momose".
  - May 27, 2023: new model designed by Anny, modelled by Otozuki Teru, rendered in Unity.
  - Dec 19, 2024: third model by Anny, rigged by Kitanya.
  - Nov 15, 2025: a 3D model by jjinomu, used in VRChat.
  - Vedal chose a VTuber avatar because "it was much easier for an AI to control" than realistic human footage.
  - Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuro-sama)

**LLM**
- Wikipedia names no model: responses are "generated by a large language model" with "low latency" that enables fast conversation — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuro-sama)
- A third-party compilation (community research, not Vedal) makes these claims:
  - Neuro runs a custom fine-tune of an undisclosed open-source base model, on Vedal's own GPUs.
  - Training data is "Twitch stream interactions, curated by Vedal". Its cited source is a Threads post: [Threads](https://www.threads.com/@sinisterpixel/post/DEa_XlXoKjK).
  - Frequently repeated figures of "~2B parameters, q2_k quantization" are marked UNVERIFIED, traced only to Fandom wikis with "no verifiable primary source".
  - Source: [Lin-Guanguo LLM memory research: Neuro-sama](https://lin-guanguo.github.io/llm-memory-research/neuro-sama.research/)
- Learning loop, per the same compilation: stream (inference only) → collect transcripts → Vedal curates by hand → offline fine-tune → redeploy. It calls this "iterative batch fine-tuning, NOT true continual learning", with human curation as "the critical bottleneck". It also says personality is baked into the weights, and system prompts carry only situational context — [Lin-Guanguo compilation](https://lin-guanguo.github.io/llm-memory-research/neuro-sama.research/)

**Voice and singing**
- TTS (community-sourced): Microsoft Azure TTS voice "Ashley", pitch-shifted up about 25%. Evil Neuro has a different voice setup — [Neuro-sama Fandom wiki, via search snippet](https://neurosama.fandom.com/wiki/Neuro-sama); repeated as "confirmed" by the [Lin-Guanguo compilation](https://lin-guanguo.github.io/llm-memory-research/neuro-sama.research/), but no Vedal primary source was found.
- Singing (community-sourced):
  - She has sung since early January 2023 through a "plugin". The first song was "Blinding Lights".
  - The singing plugin has been overhauled twice (fans call the versions V1, V2 and V3).
  - "Her singing is a separate system to her chatting."
  - Source: [Neuro-sama Fandom wiki: Karaoke, via search snippet](https://neurosama.fandom.com/wiki/Karaoke) (the page returned HTTP 402 when fetched directly)
  - Wikipedia likewise lists "singing/karaoke" among her content types: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuro-sama)

**Memory and speech-to-text**
- Memory: no documented persistent cross-session memory. There are hints of retrieval-style memory, "not publicly documented". Vedal describes memory as "an ongoing challenge" — [Lin-Guanguo compilation](https://lin-guanguo.github.io/llm-memory-research/neuro-sama.research/)
- STT is used for voice collabs; the provider is not disclosed — [Lin-Guanguo compilation](https://lin-guanguo.github.io/llm-memory-research/neuro-sama.research/)

**Filters and moderation**
- Filtered outputs display the word "filtered". Subtitles accompany every response.
- On Jan 11, 2023 the channel got a two-week Twitch ban for "hateful conduct", attributed to Holocaust denial and other offensive remarks. Vedal then improved the filters and hand-curated the training data before returning on Jan 25, 2023.
- Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuro-sama)

**Game-playing: the Neuro SDK (primary source)**
- Protocol: WebSocket. The message types are `startup`, `context`, `actions/register`, `actions/force`, `action` and `action/result`.
- Official SDKs: Unity and Godot. Community SDKs: Rust, JS/TS, Java, Lua, GameMaker, C, Python, Kotlin, **C++**, C#, Ren'Py, RPG Maker, StarCraft II.
- Tools: "Randy", a test bot that mimics the Neuro API with random actions; "Tony & Jippity" for manual and realistic simulation.
- MIT licence, about 837 stars. Last commit Aug 21, 2026 ("Add Python & JS/TS Server SDKs to README").
- Design guidance: turn-based games fit best. High-APM games or games with complex visual state integrate poorly. Good candidates are card games, visual novels and strategy titles.
- Sources: [VedalAI/neuro-sdk](https://github.com/VedalAI/neuro-sdk); [commits feed](https://github.com/VedalAI/neuro-sdk/commits.atom)
- How it works, per the compilation: games declare JSON action schemas and Neuro picks a typed action name plus JSON data. The game validates and executes it, so "Neuro does not directly control UI/keyboard". Reference repos:
  - neuro-inscryption: state serializer → validated actions → mouse automation
  - neuro-amongus: LSTM movement controller plus deterministic solvers
  - neuro-cyberpunk: high-level quest, inventory and dialogue actions
  - Source: [Lin-Guanguo compilation](https://lin-guanguo.github.io/llm-memory-research/neuro-sama.research/)
- Games played include osu!, Minecraft, Slay the Spire and GeoGuessr — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuro-sama)

**Evil Neuro, collabs and other formats**

  - Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuro-sama)

**Why she works (Vedal's own words)**
- Vedal credits "the novelty": "she can be quite chaotic, saying things humans wouldn't really say" — [Futurism](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/ai-twitch-streamer-neuro-sama)

**How open-source recreations compare**
- kimjammer/Neuro (Llama 3 8B plus CoquiTTS) reports 1-3 s latency with DeepSpeed.
- AIRIS reports about 7 s on a GTX 745 and 1-2 s on an RTX 4080.
- kimjammer found that "aligned base models stubbornly resist certain behaviors (e.g., swearing), even when explicitly prompted". That is the case for fine-tuning over prompting alone.
- Source: [Lin-Guanguo compilation](https://lin-guanguo.github.io/llm-memory-research/neuro-sama.research/)

### Inferences
- Neuro's architecture is a set of loosely coupled subsystems: chat LLM, TTS, a separate singing pipeline, a filter, game adapters, and the avatar renderer (Unity since 2023). This is the same decomposition Revia's presence/avatar event bridge already implies. Revia should keep singing, game control and talking as separate producers of avatar and audio events.
- Keeping the LLM out of low-level game input (typed actions validated by the game adapter) is directly reusable. Revia can use the Neuro SDK protocol as-is; a community C++ SDK exists, and any game with a Neuro SDK integration becomes playable. Revia could also speak the same JSON schema internally for its own tools.
- Her appeal rests on personality consistency and comedic chaos, which Vedal gets through fine-tuning on curated transcripts. A solo developer can approximate this with prompting plus later LoRA fine-tunes on curated Revia transcripts. Revia's existing session persistence and summarizer produce exactly the transcript corpus that loop needs.
- The visible "Filtered" behaviour turns a safety mechanism into a running joke. That is a cheap, well-tested pattern to copy.

### Gaps
- No primary source (Vedal statement, dev stream timestamp, or talk) was found for: base LLM and size, current TTS (the Azure Ashley claim is from the community wiki and may be outdated after 2025 model changes), how chat messages are selected, the latency budget, the filter implementation (keyword list vs. classifier vs. second LLM), or the Live2D/Unity animation control (how emotions map to expressions).
- Whether the Dec 2024 model and the Nov 2025 3D model are driven by the same animation logic is unknown.
- The singing pipeline (pre-rendered AI covers vs. live synthesis, and who produces them) could not be verified. The wiki page is paywalled to the fetch tool (HTTP 402).

## 2. Other notable AI VTubers/companions and open-source projects (stack, license, 2025-2026 activity, strengths)

### Takeaway
The open-source field has split:
- **Project AIRI** (moeru-ai, MIT, about 49.7k stars, betas shipping Aug 2026) is now the dominant "build your own Neuro". It covers Live2D and VRM, game agents, and multi-platform chat.
- **Open-LLM-VTuber** (MIT, about 13.9k stars) is the most popular Python voice-first Live2D companion. Its main branch was mostly maintenance in 2026.
- **handcrafted-persona-engine** (C#/.NET, Windows-only, MIT) is the closest architectural analogue to Revia: a native Windows app with Live2D, phoneme-based lip sync and Spout output to OBS.
- **AITuberKit** (Next.js) is the most streaming-focused kit and the most active (v2.78.1, Sep 2026), but it moved to a custom licence that requires a paid licence for commercial use.
- Older repos (AI-Waifu-Vtuber, kimjammer/Neuro, Amica) are mainly useful as reference designs.

No verified, metrics-backed list of other prominent AI VTubers besides Neuro and Evil Neuro was found.

## 3. Avatar control: VTube Studio API, Cubism SDK, VRM, lip sync, expressions, idle, gaze

### Takeaway
There are two workable routes for Live2D:
- **(a) Drive VTube Studio externally** over its local WebSocket API. Use hotkeys and expressions, inject parameters, and handle lip sync either by routing TTS audio through a virtual cable into VTS's microphone input or by injecting `MouthOpen` yourself.
- **(b) Render Live2D in-process** with the Cubism Native (C++) SDK and push frames to OBS via Spout.

For VRM, the interoperable wire format is the **VMC protocol** (OSC over UDP) into VSeeFace, Warudo or VNyan, or you render in-process yourself.

Timestamp-driven viseme lip sync (e.g. Azure viseme events, or phoneme timings from your own TTS) beats volume-based lip sync, but volume/RMS is the universal fallback.

### Cited Findings

**VTube Studio public API** ([DenchiSoft/VTubeStudio](https://github.com/DenchiSoft/VTubeStudio))
- Transport and discovery:
  - WebSocket at `ws://localhost:8001` (configurable), JSON messages.
  - VTS broadcasts its API state over UDP on port 47779 every 2 s, including the instance ID and window title.
- Authentication:
  - `AuthenticationTokenRequest` (plugin name, developer name, optional 128×128 icon) triggers a user popup and returns a token.
  - Each session then sends `AuthenticationRequest`. Tokens persist across sessions and the user can revoke them.
- Avatar-control requests:
  - `HotkeyTriggerRequest`: 5-frame cooldown per hotkey, 32-hotkey queue.
  - `ExpressionActivationRequest`: 0-2 s fade.
  - `MoveModelRequest`: smooth move over 0-2 s.
  - `ItemLoadRequest` and `MoveItemRequest`.
  - `ColorTintRequest`.
  - Physics overrides: only one plugin at a time.
  - `NDIConfigRequest`.
- Parameter injection (`InjectParameterDataRequest`):
  - `mode` is `set` (exclusive) or `add`; an optional `weight` (0-1) blends with face tracking; `faceFound` controls the tracking-lost animation.
  - **You must resend at least once per second or the parameter reverts.**
- Custom parameters (`ParameterCreationRequest`): 100 per plugin, 300 global.
- Events come through `EventSubscriptionRequest`: model load, items, tracking state, model clicks, hotkeys.
- The API has **no audio playback or lip-sync-from-audio request**.
- Common practice for lip sync is to route TTS output through a **virtual audio cable (VB-Cable)** into VTS as its microphone:
  - [AI-Waifu-Vtuber](https://github.com/ardha27/AI-Waifu-Vtuber): "VB-Cable virtual audio cable to capture desktop audio as microphone input for VtubeStudio"
  - [kimjammer/Neuro](https://github.com/kimjammer/Neuro): audio piped through a virtual cable, and VTS "handles the lip sync"

**Live2D Cubism SDK (direct rendering)**
- Lip-sync methods are documented for Native (C++), Web (TS) and Java ([Live2D docs: Lip-sync](https://docs.live2d.com/en/cubism-sdk-manual/lipsync/)):
  1. **Real-time audio level.** Read the lip-sync parameter IDs from the `model3.json` Groups (`GetLipSyncParameterCount()`), then write a 0-1 value to them (typically `ParamMouthOpenY`) with `SetParameterValue` or `AddParameterValue` before the model update.
  2. **Motion-based.** Lip sync is baked into motions, with the IDs set via `CubismMotion::SetEffectIds`.
  3. **Separate mouth-only motion manager**, so lip sync is decoupled from body motion.
  - A separate "Motion Sync" plugin exists, but its vowel/phoneme details were not documented on that page.
- Licensing ([Live2D SDK license](https://www.live2d.com/en/sdk/license/)):
  - A licence is "only required upon releasing your content". Individuals and small-scale enterprises are exempt from the publication licence and fees.
  - For VTuber tracking software used as "a primary element of a business" with annual sales over 20 million JPY, a Publication License Agreement is required.
  - The Free Material License covers sample models.

**VRM: VMC protocol, Warudo, three-vrm** ([VMC protocol](https://protocol.vmc.info/english))
- VMC is OSC over UDP. Default ports are 39539 (Performer → Marionette) and 39540 (Assistant → Performer).
- Addresses: `/VMC/Ext/Root/Pos`, `/VMC/Ext/Bone/Pos`, `/VMC/Ext/Blend/Val` plus `/VMC/Ext/Blend/Apply`, and `/VMC/Ext/OK`.
- Blendshape names differ by VRM version:
  - VRM0: `Joy, Angry, Sorrow, Fun, A, I, U, E, O, Blink_L, Blink_R`
  - VRM1: `happy, angry, sad, relaxed, aa, ih, ou, ee, oh, blinkLeft, blinkRight`
  - Senders and receivers must handle both, and must ignore unknown addresses.
- Warudo ([Warudo scripting docs](https://docs.warudo.app/docs/scripting/overview)):
  - Blueprints (visual scripting) and C# plugins (Steam Workshop, scripting playground).
  - A VMC Sender asset and VMC receiving; Stream Deck built in; a community Streamer.bot plugin.
  - "Warudo Pro" exists as a separate product; pricing was not given on the page.
- three-vrm ([pixiv/three-vrm](https://github.com/pixiv/three-vrm)): MIT, about 2.2k stars, v3. It is a GLTFLoader plugin with MToon materials, WebGPURenderer support, and needs three.js r167+. AIRI, Amica and AITuberKit use it.

**Expressions and idle behaviour**
- ChatdollKit uses inline LLM tags `[face:Joy]` and `[anim:...]` — [ChatdollKit](https://github.com/uezo/ChatdollKit)
- SillyTavern uses a separate emotion classifier that maps emotions to animations — [Extension-Live2d](https://github.com/SillyTavern/Extension-Live2d)
- Open-LLM-VTuber has "Live2D expression mapping" and touch feedback — [Open-LLM-VTuber](https://github.com/Open-LLM-VTuber/Open-LLM-VTuber)
- persona-engine has "emotion-driven expressions and motions" — [persona-engine](https://github.com/fagenorn/handcrafted-persona-engine)
- AIRI implements auto-blink, gaze tracking and idle animations for both Live2D and VRM — [AIRI](https://github.com/moeru-ai/airi)

**Viseme and phoneme lip sync** ([Microsoft Learn: viseme](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/how-to-speech-synthesis-viseme), updated 2026)
- Azure TTS emits `VisemeReceived` events: 22 viseme IDs (0 = silence, 21 = p/b/m, etc.), each with an audio offset in 100-ns ticks.
- Through the SSML `mstts:viseme` element it can also emit 3D blend-shape frames: 60 fps, 55 ARKit-style positions including `jawOpen`, `mouthFunnel` and `mouthPucker`.
- There is a C++ Speech SDK example.
- persona-engine offers VBridger (phoneme-style) or NVIDIA Audio2Face lip sync — [persona-engine](https://github.com/fagenorn/handcrafted-persona-engine)
- ChatdollKit uses uLipSync — [ChatdollKit](https://github.com/uezo/ChatdollKit)
- SillyTavern uses character-count timing, the crudest method — [Extension-Live2d](https://github.com/SillyTavern/Extension-Live2d)

**Getting frames to OBS**
- The OBS Spout2 plugin is GPL-2.0 and Windows 64-bit only. It provides a Spout input source and a Spout output filter, shares GPU textures above 1080p, and avoids screen capture — [Off-World-Live/obs-spout2-plugin](https://github.com/Off-World-Live/obs-spout2-plugin)
- persona-engine publishes avatar, subtitles and roulette as separate Spout streams — [persona-engine](https://github.com/fagenorn/handcrafted-persona-engine)
- VTS offers NDI output configurable over its API — [VTubeStudio API](https://github.com/DenchiSoft/VTubeStudio)

### Inferences (C++/Qt integration)
- **Phase 1, fastest path: VTS as renderer.** Use `QWebSocket` to `ws://localhost:8001`. Send hotkeys and expressions from Revia's emotion events. Inject custom parameters (e.g. `ReviaMouthOpen`, `ReviaMouthForm`, gaze X/Y) at 30-60 Hz: the 1 s resend rule is a minimum, and lip sync needs frame rate. Injecting mouth values straight from Revia's TTS timing avoids VB-Cable, the extra audio device, and the latency and echo problems that come with it. Keep VB-Cable as the zero-code fallback.
- **Phase 1b, self-contained route: link the Cubism Native SDK.** It is C++ with OpenGL/D3D renderers and can sit in a `QOpenGLWidget` or QRhi-backed window. Write lip-sync values directly and publish the render target with the Spout2 SDK, which OBS receives with alpha. This removes the VTS dependency and licence (VTS is a paid Steam app for commercial use; not verified here). You then own idle, blink, breath, physics and gaze, all of which the SDK samples provide.
- **Phase 2, VRM.** The cheapest route is to emit VMC/OSC from Revia over `QUdpSocket` with a small OSC encoder: blendshapes `aa/ih/ou/ee/oh`, emotions, and head/eye bones for gaze and idle. Point it at Warudo, VSeeFace or VNyan, which handle rendering, physics and output to OBS. The alternative is in-process rendering (e.g. a Qt 3D/QRhi glTF loader plus custom MToon and spring bones, which is a large job) or embedding three-vrm in QtWebEngine.
- **One abstraction for both.** Define Revia's avatar events once, e.g. `emotion{name,intensity,fade}`, `viseme{id,t}`, `mouthOpen{v}`, `gaze{x,y}`, `gesture{name}`. Then write per-backend adapters: VTS WebSocket, Cubism-native, VMC. Viseme IDs map to Live2D (`ParamMouthOpenY` plus `ParamMouthForm`) or VRM (`aa/ih/ou/ee/oh`) through a small table.
- **Lip-sync quality order:** TTS phoneme/viseme timestamps > audio-driven viseme estimation (uLipSync/VBridger-style) > RMS volume > text-length timing. If Revia's local TTS gives no timestamps, run RMS on the playback buffer with attack/release smoothing as the baseline.

## 4. Streaming integration: Twitch, YouTube, OBS, multi-platform chat, message selection, latency

### Takeaway
All three platform integrations are plain WebSocket/HTTPS, so all are feasible from C++/Qt:
- **Twitch:** EventSub over WebSocket (`channel.chat.message` plus monetization and moderation events) and Helix for sending.
- **YouTube:** `liveChatMessages.streamList` (server-streaming) or `liveChatMessages.list` polling at 1 quota unit per call.
- **OBS:** obs-websocket v5 for scenes, captions and text sources.

Message selection is a product decision. The open-source projects either read "recent messages" or talk on their own when chat is quiet; none publishes a sophisticated selector.

### Cited Findings

**Twitch chat** ([Twitch chat docs](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/chat/))
- "The preferred method of viewing and sending chats on Twitch is through EventSub and Twitch API." IRC remains available but "has limited features". The page gives **no IRC deprecation timeline**.
- Subscribing to `channel.chat.message` is what joins the chat room.
- The Send Chat Message API has its own rate-limit bucket.
- Bot badge: send with an App Access Token, have `channel:bot` authorized by the broadcaster (or be a moderator), and not be the broadcaster.
- Rate limits: 20 messages / 30 s for regular accounts (100 as mod/VIP), and 1 message / s per channel. Verified bots get 7,500 / 30 s.

**Twitch EventSub subscription types** ([Twitch EventSub types](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/eventsub/eventsub-subscription-types/))

| Subscription | Version | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| `channel.chat.message`, `.notification`, `.clear`, `.message_delete` | 1 | `user:read:chat` |
| `channel.follow` | 2 | `moderator:read:followers` |
| `channel.subscribe`, `channel.subscription.message`, `channel.subscription.gift` | 1 | `channel:read:subscriptions` |
| `channel.cheer`, `channel.bits.use` | 1 | `bits:read` |

**EventSub over WebSocket** ([Twitch EventSub WebSockets](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/eventsub/handling-websocket-events/))
- URL: `wss://eventsub.wss.twitch.tv/ws`, with `keepalive_timeout_seconds` between 10 and 600.
- You must subscribe within 10 s of the Welcome message.
- Limits per user token: max 3 connections, 300 subscriptions per connection, total cost 10.
- WebSocket transport requires **user** access tokens; app tokens fail.
- On a reconnect message, connect to the new URL within 30 s and keep the old socket open until the new Welcome arrives.

**YouTube live chat**
- `liveChatMessages.streamList` "establishes a server-streaming connection that lets you receive live chat messages for a specific chat with low latency". Docs list gRPC error codes — [YouTube streamList](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/live/docs/liveChatMessages/streamList)
- The `list` docs now say: "To poll for live chat messages, use the `liveChatMessages.streamList` method… helps to avoid exceeding your quota" — [YouTube list](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/live/docs/liveChatMessages/list)
- `list` details: `maxResults` 200-2000 (default 500), `pollingIntervalMillis` tells the client how long to wait, and `pageToken`/`nextPageToken` carry continuation — [YouTube list](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/live/docs/liveChatMessages/list)
- Quota: the official quota table (checked directly) lists these costs — [YouTube quota costs](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/determine_quota_cost)

  | Method | Units |
  |---|---|
  | `liveChatMessages.list` | 1 |
  | `liveChatMessages.insert` / `.delete` | 50 |
  | `liveChatBans.insert` / `.delete` | 50 |

  | `liveBroadcasts.list` | 1 |
  | Default daily quota | 10,000 units |

  - A third-party claim (surfaced in search, source not identified) that polling costs 5 units per request **conflicts** with the official table.
  - streamList's quota cost is not documented on its page.
- A community source says streamList also has a REST form, `GET https://youtube.googleapis.com/youtube/v3/liveChat/messages/stream`, so gRPC/HTTP2 is not required. This is unverified — [search result citing community implementations, e.g. Voidscape Social-Feed PR](https://github.com/Voidscape-Development/Social-Feed/pull/2); a gRPC example exists at [dipaadipati/yt-livechat-grpc](https://github.com/dipaadipati/yt-livechat-grpc).
- Hobby projects often skip the API: AI-Waifu-Vtuber reads YouTube chat via pytchat (a scraper) and Twitch via IRC — [AI-Waifu-Vtuber](https://github.com/ardha27/AI-Waifu-Vtuber). AITuberKit reads YouTube comments via the API or the OneComme aggregator — [AITuberKit](https://github.com/tegnike/aituber-kit)

**OBS WebSocket v5** ([obs-websocket protocol.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/obsproject/obs-websocket/master/docs/generated/protocol.md))
- OpCodes: Hello 0, Identify 1, Identified 2, Reidentify 3, Event 5, Request 6, RequestResponse 7, RequestBatch 8, RequestBatchResponse 9.
- Auth: base64(sha256(password + salt)), then base64(sha256(secret + challenge)).
- Events are selected with an `eventSubscriptions` bitmask. High-volume events such as `InputVolumeMeters` (bit 1<<16) are opt-in.
- Confirmed requests:
  - `SetCurrentProgramScene`, `SetSceneItemEnabled`
  - `SetInputSettings` (text sources), `SendStreamCaption`
  - `TriggerMediaInputAction`, `SetInputAudioMonitorType`, `PressInputPropertiesButton`
  - `StartStream`, `StopStream`, `GetStreamStatus`
  - `BroadcastCustomEvent`, `CallVendorRequest`
- The default port (4455) was not stated in the generated doc fetched; it comes from general knowledge.

**Chat selection in practice**
- kimjammer/Neuro: "reading recent chat messages". A prompter decides when to respond from several signals (new chat, silence, and so on). Prompt pieces are "injections" sorted by priority, with the highest priority placed last in the prompt — [kimjammer/Neuro](https://github.com/kimjammer/Neuro)
- AITuberKit: speaks on its own when there are no comments ("conversation continuation mode") — [AITuberKit](https://github.com/tegnike/aituber-kit)
- Open-LLM-VTuber: "proactive speaking" and a Bilibili danmaku client — [Open-LLM-VTuber](https://github.com/Open-LLM-VTuber/Open-LLM-VTuber)

**Latency**
- Community recreations report 1-3 s (kimjammer with DeepSpeed) and 1-2 s on an RTX 4080 (AIRIS) — [Lin-Guanguo compilation](https://lin-guanguo.github.io/llm-memory-research/neuro-sama.research/)
- Open-LLM-VTuber had "streaming TTS for reduced latency" planned for v1.3-v1.4 — [Open-LLM-VTuber releases](https://github.com/Open-LLM-VTuber/Open-LLM-VTuber/releases)

### Inferences
- **Twitch from Qt:**
  - Use `QWebSocket` to EventSub with a user token covering `user:read:chat`, `bits:read`, `channel:read:subscriptions`, `moderator:read:followers`, channel-points read, `moderator:manage:automod` and `channel:bot`.
  - Send chat with `QNetworkAccessManager` → Helix Send Chat Message.
  - OAuth: device-code or localhost-redirect flow, with the token stored in Windows Credential Manager.
  - Skip IRC for new code.
- **YouTube from Qt:**
  - Start with `liveChatMessages.list` polling that honours `pollingIntervalMillis`. At 1 unit per call, 5 s polling for 4 h is about 2,900 units, well inside 10k per day.
  - Add streamList (the REST streaming GET if confirmed, otherwise gRPC via grpc++) later.
  - Keep sends to a minimum: each `insert` costs 50 units, so about 200 bot messages per day uses the entire quota.
- **Aggregation:**
  - Normalize every platform into one `ChatEvent{platform, userId, displayName, roles[mod, sub, vip, member], text, paidAmount, eventType}` queue.
  - Paid and special events (bits, subs, raids, channel points, Super Chats, memberships) go into a separate priority lane that always gets a response or acknowledgement.
- **Selection strategy** (none published by Neuro; this is a synthesis):
  1. Hard filter: blocklist, AutoMod-held messages, links, duplicates, flood.
  2. Per-user cooldown.
  3. Score messages by recency, directedness (mentions Revia or asks a question), novelty against recent topics, supporter status, and platform fairness.
  4. Sample from the top-k, with some randomness so it doesn't feel scripted.
  5. Batch similar messages ("chat is asking about X").
  6. When chat is quiet, fall back to proactive topics or games.
- **Latency budget** for chat text (no ASR):
  - Selection: ~0 s
  - LLM time-to-first-sentence: ~0.3-1 s locally
  - First-sentence TTS: ~0.2-0.5 s
  - Platform stream delay: typically several seconds (not verified here) dominates chat-to-reaction time
  - Aim for ≤1.5 s from message pick to voice start. Stream sentence-by-sentence TTS; OBS captions via `SendStreamCaption` or a text source updated per sentence.
- **Frames:** Spout (persona-engine pattern) or a VTS NDI/virtual camera get the avatar into OBS. Revia's own output should be a Spout sender with alpha, plus optional separate Spout senders for subtitles and widgets.

## 5. Safety and moderation for live AI streams

### Takeaway
The main precedent is Neuro-sama's 2-week Twitch ban in January 2023 for "hateful conduct" caused by unfiltered LLM output. The fix was layered: an output filter (visible "Filtered"), curated training data, and subtitles.

As of Sept 2026, Twitch has **no AI-disclosure rule** (the CEO says one would come only if needed). YouTube requires disclosure only for *realistic* synthetic content, which a clearly animated AI VTuber generally is not, but a cloned real person's voice would be. The streamer remains responsible for what the AI says. Chat is untrusted input that can carry prompt injection.

### Cited Findings
**The Neuro-sama precedent**
- Two-week ban from Jan 11, 2023 for "hateful conduct" (Holocaust denial and other offensive remarks). Vedal "improved filters and manually curated training data" before returning Jan 25, 2023.
- Filters target racist statements, profanity and Holocaust denial, and filtered lines display "filtered". Subtitles accompany responses.
- Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuro-sama)
- Futurism (Jan 2026) still cites the Holocaust-denial incident as the defining moderation risk of autonomous AI streamers — [Futurism](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/ai-twitch-streamer-neuro-sama)

**Open-source filtering practice**
- kimjammer/Neuro: `blacklist.txt`, with the warning that it cannot guarantee "non-vile responses" — [kimjammer/Neuro](https://github.com/kimjammer/Neuro)
- persona-engine ships "profanity filtering" — [persona-engine](https://github.com/fagenorn/handcrafted-persona-engine)
- Aligned base models resist some behaviours even when prompted. This matters in both directions: an edgy persona is harder to get, and base alignment gives a safety floor — [Lin-Guanguo compilation](https://lin-guanguo.github.io/llm-memory-research/neuro-sama.research/)

**Platform moderation hooks**
- Twitch: `automod.message.hold` (scope `moderator:manage:automod`) lets a bot see AutoMod-held messages. Chat clear and message-delete events are also available — [Twitch EventSub types](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/eventsub/eventsub-subscription-types/)
- YouTube: `liveChatBans.insert` and `liveChatMessages.delete` cost 50 quota units each — [YouTube quota costs](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/determine_quota_cost)

**Disclosure rules**
- YouTube requires disclosure when realistic AI content "makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't do", "alters footage of a real event or place", or "generates a realistic scene that didn't actually occur".
- It does **not** require disclosure for fantastical or "fully animated" content, AI-assisted scripts, captions, or "cloning one's own voice".
- Persistent non-disclosure can lead to a label being applied, content removal, or YPP suspension. The page does not specifically address livestreams.
- Source: [YouTube Help: altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- Twitch CEO Dan Clancy (Sep 20, 2026) is skeptical of pre-emptive AI-disclosure rules: "If this happened, we would absolutely do something like that", but AI substitution "just isn't happening". Twitch has used AI in AutoMod since 2013 — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/twitch-ceo-dan-clancy-says-platform-doesnt-need-ai-disclosure-rules-for-now/)
- Twitch prohibits synthetic non-consensual explicit imagery (deepfakes) — [Twitch Safety: Addressing Explicit Deepfake Content, title from search results](https://safety.twitch.tv/s/article/Addressing-Explicit-Deepfake-Content?language=en_US)

**Asset licensing**
- Live2D sample models are under the Free Material License, with restrictions for medium and large enterprises — [Open-LLM-VTuber](https://github.com/Open-LLM-VTuber/Open-LLM-VTuber); [Live2D SDK license](https://www.live2d.com/en/sdk/license/)
- AITuberKit needs a commercial licence for commercial use — [AITuberKit](https://github.com/tegnike/aituber-kit)

### Inferences
- **Defense in depth for Revia live mode:**
  1. **Input:** platform AutoMod plus Revia's own blocklist and regex; strip or neutralize instruction-like text; wrap every chat message as quoted data with the username, never as system or instruction text; cap length.
  2. **Model:** a streaming-specific system prompt with firm rules on real people, politics, slurs and self-harm; no tool or function access reachable from chat except whitelisted, parameter-validated "chat games" (Neuro SDK-style typed actions).
  3. **Output:** a sentence-level filter before TTS, i.e. a keyword list plus a fast local classifier or small moderation LLM. A blocked sentence becomes "Filtered" (Neuro's pattern, which doubles as a comedy beat) and is never spoken.
  4. **Operator:** a hotkey or Stream Deck kill switch that mutes TTS, clears the queue and swaps to a BRB scene via obs-websocket `SetCurrentProgramScene`, plus a live transcript log for audits.
  5. **Delay:** optionally, a few seconds of audio/speech buffer so the operator or classifier can veto.
- **Prompt injection through chat** ("ignore previous instructions…", impersonating the dev, asking her to read out secrets or links) is the main new attack surface. Never put secrets, API keys or file paths in the live prompt. Treat usernames as untrusted, since they too can carry injection text.
- **Disclosure:** an animated original character with a synthetic voice likely needs no YouTube label. Still, add "AI VTuber" to channel bios and titles for transparency, and never clone a real person's voice without consent (the YouTube rule plus general risk). Twitch had no AI rule as of Sept 2026, but its general community guidelines (hateful conduct, harassment) apply to what the AI says, as the Neuro ban shows.

### Gaps
- There are no primary sources on Neuro-sama's current filter design.
- Documented prompt-injection incidents on AI VTuber streams could not be researched: the search budget was exhausted.
- Twitch Community Guidelines text on bots and automated or unattended streams, and any 2026 Twitch or YouTube rule changes on AI-generated livestreams, were not fetched.
- YouTube's rules on repetitive or "inauthentic" AI content for monetization (YPP) were not checked.

## 6. Entertainment formats that work for AI VTubers

### Takeaway
Neuro-sama's portfolio is the template:
- games through structured APIs (osu!, Minecraft, Inscryption, Slay the Spire, GeoGuessr, Buckshot Roulette)
- karaoke with a separate singing pipeline
- reactions
- dev streams where the creator is the human foil
- collabs with human VTubers and creators
- a rival or sibling AI (Evil Neuro)
- IRL gimmicks
- subathons with sub goals, which produced Twitch-record hype trains

Open-source kits add slide narration, game commentary from screenshots, multi-AI dialogue, and chat-driven roulette and minigames.

  - AIRI: Minecraft and Factorio agents — [AIRI](https://github.com/moeru-ai/airi)

### Inferences
- **Formats a solo developer can build, in rough order of effort:**
  1. Just chatting with good chat selection, plus proactive topics during quiet periods.
  2. Reacting to videos or screens via screenshot vision (already in Revia's roadmap).
  3. Chat games tied to channel points or bits: roulette, trivia, "Revia decides", polls and predictions via EventSub.
  4. Karaoke from pre-rendered AI covers played with synced lip sync. This is the cheapest singing path and matches Neuro's separate-system design; model choice is covered by the other researcher.
  5. Turn-based games through Neuro SDK-compatible adapters.
  6. Collabs, where the human speaks over Discord or a mic and Revia uses ASR with turn-taking.
  7. A second persona ("Evil Revia") reusing the same stack with a different prompt, voice and model.
  8. Subathon mechanics: timer extensions from subs, bits and Super Chats, with milestones unlocking content.
- **The creator-as-foil dynamic** (Vedal on dev streams) is central to Neuro's charm and costs nothing extra technically. Revia's developer appearing as co-host is a strong format.
- **Cross-posting** to Twitch and YouTube simultaneously means doubled chat. The aggregated queue with per-platform fairness (section 4) matters more than for single-platform streamers.
